❯ FORECAST METHOD

What a reset probability actually means

The number on Codex Resets estimates the chance of an observed global reset event over the next 24 or 48 hours. It is a forecast, not a claim that a reset has been scheduled or confirmed.

Updated 30 August 2026 · 3 minute read

History supplies the baseline

The model starts from verified past reset episodes and the spacing between them. A probability curve then describes how likely another broad event appears within a future time window. It does not use a user’s personal account data.

Read the number first, then the time

The prominent 24- or 48-hour number is the overall forecast. The timing chart does not introduce another percentage: its highlighted three-hour block simply marks the likeliest time inside that selected window.

Signals can move the estimate

The dashboard monitors a limited set of public signals, such as relevant public posts and official service status. A signal is not treated as a reset confirmation by itself. Direct-source evidence is stabilized before it can affect the verified history or trigger a notification.

Uncertainty is part of the result

  • A higher probability is not a promise that a reset will happen.
  • A low probability does not rule out an unexpected change.
  • Only a confirmed event can enter the public history. An optional email forecast can be sent once when the next-24-hour planning likelihood reaches 70%, and a direct official schedule can send a due-time notice; neither is a reset confirmation.
The forecast is intentionally conservative. It is useful for deciding whether flexible work can wait, not for making a critical deadline depend on a reset.
